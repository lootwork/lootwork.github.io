Why this role matters at Pennylane

As Chief of Staff to the CTO, you operate as a senior leader in the Tech organisation. Your leverage does not come from managing teams but from owning the hardest problems that sit between them, and from being technical enough to earn the trust of the other engineering leaders.

You are the person the CTO trusts with ambiguous, org-wide problems. You turn that ambiguity into a clear framing, a decision, an owner and a measurable outcome.

This is not a coordination role. You are expected to have opinions on engineering strategy, staffing choices and technical trade-offs, and to defend them in front of the Tech leadership team.

Team and Context

Pennylane's Tech organisation is 70+ squads, each led by an Engineering Manager working with a PM and a Product Designer. We serve 1.5m+ customers and we want to scale in Europe. At that size, the hard problems are no longer inside a squad: how the organisation plans, how it decides, how it measures itself, how it invests in foundations without stalling product delivery. That is your perimeter.

You report directly to Quentin, Co-founder & CTO. Your influence comes from the scope of what you own and from credibility with senior engineering leaders, which is why we ask for a technical background. As the organisation grows, this role can evolve towards a management position.

Key missions

Engineering strategy and transversal initiatives

- Own the strategic, cross-organization initiatives that no single squad can carry: platform or architecture programmes, reliability and quality step-changes, engineering productivity, technical debt paydown

- Frame the problem, build the technical and business case, secure alignment across Engineering, Product and Security to drive execution

- Lead the Tech side of company-level programmes (e.g. European expansion, major compliance or infrastructure shifts) alongside the rest of the Tech leadership

Planning & Performance tracking

- Own the Tech planning cycle: collect the inputs, surface the trade-offs (capacity vs. platform, run vs. new bets), and drive the arbitration with Tech leadership

- Drive the definition and calibration of Tech OKRs, and hold the organisation accountable

- Define what "good" looks like for a 70+ squad engineering organization and build the metrics that measure it (delivery cadence, reliability, quality, staffing and attrition, engineering satisfaction)

Decision-making and leadership cadence

- Own the Tech leadership operating system: staff meetings, QBRs, roadmap and architecture reviews. You design them, set the agenda, write or commission the pre-reads, frame the decisions to be made, and make sure decisions land

- Maintain the CTO's decision log: what was decided, by whom, with what trade-off, and what happened next

- Raise the quality of decision-making across the Tech leadership team

Communication

- Lead internal and operational communications across the engineering organization, building clear messaging systems from board decks down to team-wide updates
